The NSE in Python · Part 2 · 12 October 2026 · 7 min read

13 years of the NSE in one chart.

From 2013 to 2025 the Nairobi stock market had two great runs and one long fall. Here is every year, the deepest drop and what the shilling did to returns, measured with the official indices.

By Michael Hawi Onyango, AI engineer and SEO/GEO specialist · Data checked against public market reports

Key facts

  • 13 years: the NSE All Share Index went from 95.55 (2 Jan 2013) to 186.58 (31 Dec 2025), +95% before dividends.
  • Up 7 years, down 6. Best year 2025 (+51.1%), worst year 2023 (−27.7%).
  • Deepest fall: −56.5% from the April 2018 high (196.57) to the November 2023 low (85.54).
  • NSE-20: from 4,140 to 3,139 over the same period (-24%), still below its 2015 peak of 5,499.64.
  • Currency matters: in 2023 Kenyan shares lost 21.9% in shillings but 38.6% in US dollars.

In Part 1 we loaded 13 years of daily NSE prices. The same files hold the market’s official indices. Unlike individual stocks, the indices already account for splits and bonus issues, so they are the cleanest way to answer the simple question: how did the Nairobi stock market actually do?

Every year at a glance

The pattern is not a steady climb. Strong years (2013, 2017, 2024, 2025) are followed by long stretches of losses. The four years from 2020 to 2023 included three losing years, ending with the two worst back to back: −23.4% in 2022 and −27.7% in 2023.

The whole period in one line

The index peaked at 196.57 on 5 April 2018. It then spent more than five years falling, reaching 85.54 on 7 November 2023, a -56.5% drop. Someone who invested in the whole market at the 2018 high would still have been underwater at the end of 2025, even after the strongest two-year rally in the dataset. It came close: the index reached 192.89 in November 2025.

The same market in dollars

For anyone outside Kenya, or comparing Kenya with other markets, the shilling matters as much as the shares. The Zamara Kenya Equity Index is published in both currencies from July 2018, so we can compare them directly.

The gap opens in 2023, when the shilling weakened sharply against the dollar: Kenyan equities lost 21.9% in shillings but 38.6% in dollars. In 2024 the currency recovered and the effect reversed: +61.0% in shillings became +95.4% in dollars. Over the full period since 9 July 2018, the index is up 103% in shillings but 59% in dollars.

The code

# Using the cleaned `nse` table from Part 1
nasi = nse[nse["code"] == "^NASI"].set_index("date")["close"]

year_end = nasi.resample("YE").last()
annual = year_end.pct_change()
annual.iloc[0] = year_end.iloc[0] / nasi.iloc[0] - 1   # 2013 from first trading day
print((annual * 100).round(1))

peak = nasi[:"2023"].idxmax()
low = nasi[peak:].idxmin()
print(peak.date(), nasi[peak], low.date(), nasi[low], f"{nasi[low] / nasi[peak] - 1:.1%}")

What this means

  • Timing dominated returns. Over 13 years the whole market’s price rose 95%, but the result for an investor depended heavily on when they started.
  • Price is not the full return. Many NSE companies pay generous dividends, which these figures leave out. Part 3 adds them back.
  • Check the currency. For foreign investors, or anyone comparing markets, the exchange rate can double a loss or a gain.

Frequently asked questions

How did the Nairobi Securities Exchange perform from 2013 to 2025?

The NSE All Share Index (NASI) went from 95.55 on 2 January 2013 to 186.58 on 31 December 2025, a gain of 95% in price terms, not counting dividends. It rose in 7 of the 13 years and fell in 6.

What was the NSE’s worst year between 2013 and 2025?

2023. The All Share Index fell 27.7% to 92.11, after a 23.4% fall in 2022. From its April 2018 high to its November 2023 low it lost 56.5%.

What was the NSE’s best year between 2013 and 2025?

2025. The All Share Index rose 51.1% to 186.58 and the NSE-20 rose 56.1% to 3,139.19, after gains of about 34% for both in 2024.

Why do NSE returns look different in US dollars?

Because the Kenyan shilling’s value changes. In 2023, Kenyan equities lost 21.9% in shillings but 38.6% in dollars as the shilling weakened. In 2024 the shilling recovered, so dollar returns (+95.4%) beat shilling returns (+61.0%).

What is the difference between NASI and the NSE-20?

The NSE All Share Index includes every listed company, weighted by market value, so large companies such as Safaricom dominate it. The NSE-20 tracks 20 selected large companies and gives each one equal weight, so Safaricom counts no more than any other member and the index can tell a different story.
